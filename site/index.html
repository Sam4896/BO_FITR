    As mentioned in Section 4.1 of the paper and on Page 3 of this project page, the acquisition-function gradient can be represented as Aᵀu.

    For a scalar Gaussian predictive posterior, the two rows of A are the Fisher-scaled input gradients of the predictive mean and variance. The vector u contains the corresponding acquisition derivatives.

    Define the real symmetric positive-semidefinite companion matrix M = AAᵀ; in particular, it is Hermitian. It is only 2 × 2 and shares the same nonzero eigenvalues as the pullback tensor G = AᵀA. Rayleigh's quotient therefore gives the spectral acquisition-gradient bound using the eigenvalues of either M or the nonzero spectrum of G.

    At an interior stationary point, ∇α = 0. On the branch where u ≠ 0 and the predictive gradients are nonzero, this forces the smallest eigenvalue to vanish and M to have rank one. Its two rows are collinear; for EI and UCB their positive acquisition derivatives require the mean and variance gradients to point in opposite directions.

    Click plot D to inspect the gradient directions at any location. To inspect a stationary point, select one of the labelled points in plot D or a button in the stationary-points box below.

    So what? For the nonzero-gradient branch of an interior stationary point, the mean and variance gradients are collinear and point in opposite directions. Then A and M = AAᵀ become rank one, the companion ellipse collapses to a line, and the acquisition vector lies in the null direction. Points where both predictive gradients vanish form a separate branch.

    From the bound to a search region

    The pullback Fisher tensor describes how the predictive distribution changes locally. FITR uses its diagonal to set the relative widths of an axis-aligned trust region.

    Regularization limits stretching in insensitive directions. Volume normalization separates the region's shape from its overall size.

    Beyond lengthscale-based models

    The weights require predictive derivatives, not kernel lengthscales. The construction also applies to other differentiable probabilistic surrogates with a valid Fisher metric.

    Try the IBNN kernel and move the center. It has no coordinate lengthscale parameters; FITR still computes its shape from the predictive derivatives.

    What is computed in these two plots?

    Both plots use the exact pullback trace of an SE GP with one observation at the center of the unit cube, observed output 1, signal variance 1, and observation-noise variance 0.1. Each point averages 64 deterministic queries. Global queries are uniform in the cube. Local queries perturb min(20, D) distinct coordinates by at most 0.25 around the observation. This fixed-size local sampling illustrates RAASP; it does not reproduce every RAASP implementation.

    The second plot uses the same global queries under four lengthscale rules: fixed scaling with p = 0, a representative subcritical rule p = 0.25, the critical rule p = 0.5, and a representative supercritical rule p = 0.75, with lengthscale 0.35 times D to the power p. The lengthscales are set directly, not learned from a prior. Values below 10 to the power of -7 are shown at the bottom of the log-scale plots. These are controlled illustrations, not BO performance comparisons or universal finite-dimensional orderings.

    The takeaway

    Two factors govern the acquisition gradient.

    The posterior map determines how the predictive distribution changes with the input. The acquisition function determines how its expected utility responds to that predictive change. A small upper bound forces a small gradient; a large upper bound still permits a zero gradient when mean and variance pulls oppose and cancel.

    What is this example & numerical setup?

    Pages 2–3 use five fixed observations, a zero-mean SE Gaussian process (signal variance 1, observation noise variance 0.0025), and latent predictive variance at the query point. Page 1 illustrates an iterative BO loop starting from three observations. Page 4 uses a controlled one-observation GP to compare trace decay. Pages 5–7 use the existing 2D geometry examples and trust-region comparison.

    Where does the geometry enter?

    The mean–variance plane is a coordinate chart for the scalar Gaussian predictive manifold. The Fisher–Rao metric measures local distributional change on that manifold, and its pullback through the posterior map measures input-space sensitivity.
